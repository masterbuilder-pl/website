## This is my biggest LEGO build so far — the town hall of Gliwice.

<img src="/public/ratusz/image18.jpg" alt="Gliwice town hall LEGO model" class="post-image" />

The idea came to me in early September. Walking through the market square, I thought: "What if I built this out of LEGO?" Back home I immediately sat down at the computer, launched Bricklink Studio, and started designing. One of the biggest challenges was getting the proportions right. I couldn't just build by eye — one brick too many here, two too few there, one window slightly smaller, another slightly larger. I had to settle on a proper scale.

### First idea — minifigure scale.

And so, just a week later, I had the ground floor designed.

<img src="/public/ratusz/image5.jpg" alt="Ground floor design" class="post-image" />

I moved on to the actual build. I pulled out my bricks and started construction on a 48×48 baseplate. Unfortunately I had to stop fairly quickly — I didn't have the pieces I needed in the right quantities. The model doesn't use many different element types, but it uses enormous numbers of the same ones. Even when I bought several hundred identical bricks it turned out to still not be enough. After placing several orders I was finally able to continue.

<img src="/public/ratusz/image2.jpg" alt="First and second floor in progress" class="post-image" />

Around mid-October I started on the first and second floors. Here too I needed to reorder elements — for example I used 72 grey 2×3 tiles just on those floors, even though most of that section is white. But that's nothing — I ordered 350 windows and 350 grilles.

### The windows alone cost around 200 PLN.

Fortunately those floors were fairly straightforward to build. The only real problem was the front wall, where I had to figure out how to attach the grey tiles in the middle. The worst was still to come though…

The roof. I spent a month and a half on it. Because of its size, unusual shape, and the tower that made the front section extremely difficult to work on, I had to redo it dozens of times. I changed the concept completely several times, which meant tearing the whole thing down and starting over. I had to get the angles exactly right, leave an opening for the tower, and at the same time create a smooth transition between the large and smaller roof sections.

The tower was the final stage and — to my surprise — one of the easier ones. The work went smoothly, I made only a few small adjustments and didn't run into any major difficulties.

### There were a few elements that turned out to be genuinely problematic and couldn't be solved with LEGO bricks alone.

The best example is the clocks. They do exist as printed bricks or stickers, but not in the right format, so I decided to make them myself. I generated a clock graphic, printed it on sticker paper, and applied it to the model. The result came out surprisingly well. While I was at it I prepared a few additional stickers — with the Master Builder logo, a QR code, and the website URL — which I used on the display base.

<img src="/public/ratusz/naklejki.jpg" alt="Custom stickers for the model" class="post-image" />

Even so, the whole structure still looked rather gloomy. I went back to photos of the real town hall and added some flowers. That helped, but it still lacked life and character.

### So I decided to add something unexpected.

<img src="/public/ratusz/image3.jpg" alt="Rooftop details" class="post-image" />

On the roof I placed Santa Claus in a sleigh pulled by a reindeer, along with Mrs. Claus clinging tightly to the runners. That small detail added a story and effectively broke up the monotony of the roof. Finally I added a falcon from LEGO Collectible Minifigures Series 24, along with a few figures on the balcony. At that point the whole thing looked exactly the way I wanted — much more interesting and full of life.

## Around 3500 bricks in total, across many colours and sizes.

### The model stands about 13.5 minifigures tall — roughly 65 cm — and sits on a 48×48 baseplate, giving it approximately 38 cm in length and width.

<img src="/public/ratusz/image17.jpg" alt="Scale comparison" class="post-image" />

I sourced the bricks in several ways. Most orders went through Bricklink, where you can get elements in large quantities and often at good prices. I also occasionally bought bricks by weight at various LEGO stores — fast and convenient, but risky since they don't always have what you need.

As a last resort I used the LEGO website's Pick a Brick. Prices there are higher, so it only makes sense for single hard-to-find elements like grey tiles. The upside is a huge selection in many colours. The downside is a delivery time of 2–3 weeks, which significantly slowed my work.

The whole project took me several months: waiting for bricks, constant new ideas, modifications, the endless roof problems… Even so, I enjoyed every bit of it. I experimented with various new — sometimes outright "illegal" — building techniques. It consumed an enormous amount of time, but I have absolutely no regrets about taking it on.
